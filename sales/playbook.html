The Fortitude Creative Sales Playbook

A sales system that starts with the diagnosis. Founder authority transfers through artifacts, not personality, so Taylor and Layla can get the same results without improvising. Brand and marketing are one pipeline. Strategy comes before visuals. Trust affects conversion at every stage.

Core positioning (memorize this)

Most home service companies buy branding and marketing as separate things: a logo from one guy, a site from another, ads from a third. Nothing compounds. We start with research on who lives in your ZIP codes, what they're worried about, what your competitors already own in their minds, and what's left for you. That becomes the strategy. The strategy becomes the brand. The brand goes on everything a homeowner or a tech sees: trucks, site, Google, ads, recruiting. The point isn't looking nicer. It's that more of the traffic you already pay for turns into booked, closed, profitable work.
Market→Positioning→Brand→Trust→Conversion→Booking→Close→Revenue

Qualification gate

Best-fit signals

  • Revenue $750K–$10M+. Set the floor from the last 15 closed deals, not by assumption.
  • 3+ trucks, or a clear growth plan
  • Decision-maker on the call, with authority
  • Pain with a specific deadline attached
  • Spending on marketing now, or ready to
  • Operationally ready: phones answered, CSRs scripted, capacity for more leads

Disqualify

  • Only wants "a cheap logo"
  • Expects guaranteed lead counts
  • Shopping purely on price
  • Too small with no growth plan

Ops gate (route to capture first)

  • Missed-call rate >15%
  • Google rating <4.2 with <15 reviews in the last 90 days
  • No documented CSR scripting
  • No review-generation system

The directive listed the four ops items as disqualifiers. It also said, under Operational Readiness, to sell those same shops a capture fix and schedule the brand later. Both can't be true, so they're split here. Ops failures mean not yet. Fit failures mean no.

When you disqualify, say it out loud.

At your size, I'd spend that money on a CSR and a service van before hiring us.

That sentence gets more referrals than any close.

Pre-call research (time-boxed)

Before Call 1 · 10 minutes
  • Website speed, mobile UX, above-fold clarity
  • Google Business Profile: rating, review count, recency, owner response rate
  • One competitor comparison (review count, brand quality)
  • One specific observation to open with
After a qualified Call 1 · 45–60 minutes
  • SEMrush: organic visibility, paid presence, competitor dominance
  • Map pack: who ranks for "AC repair [city]", their review counts, LSA badges
  • Competitor review mining: sort negative reviews into categories (no-shows, surprise pricing, rude techs)
  • ZIP demographics: median home age, household income, owner-occupancy rate
  • Trust Signal Scorecard (1–5 each): brand consistency, visual professionalism, review credibility, website clarity, mobile UX, CTA strength, proof/guarantees, local relevance, technician photos, truck alignment
  • Top 3 holes, top 3 opportunities, primary leak category

For a paid audit or after the sale, the full research becomes the deliverable. The output of the 45–60 minutes is the Prospect Snapshot.

Call 1: Discovery (20–30 min)

Rule: No teaching until you have three numbers: spend or cost per lead, booking rate, and close rate. Ticket mix is the fourth. Talk ratio: the prospect talks 60%+ of the time, you talk 40% or less.

Required questions, in order

  1. What made you reach out now?
  2. Where do jobs come from today: repeat/referral, paid, or organic?
  3. What do you spend on marketing monthly, and do you know your cost per lead or cost per booked job?
  4. What's your service ticket? Install ticket? Roughly what percent are installs?
  5. What's your booking rate? Define it: appointments set ÷ real leads, junk removed.
  6. What's your close rate?
  7. What's your gross margin, if you know it?
  8. What percentage of service customers turn into a replacement within two years? Do you sell memberships?
  9. When the phone rings during a job and nobody picks up, what happens to that call?
  10. How many techs are you running, and how hard is hiring right now?
  11. What did you try before that didn't work, and why do you think it didn't? Persona tell: "hired an agency" = delegator; "did it ourselves" = controller.
  12. Who else is involved in a decision like this?
  13. What's your timeline: this quarter or next year?

To close Call 1: read their situation back in their own words. Only book Call 2 with the secondary decision-maker on the calendar invite.

Single-call exception

Every one of these must be true:

  • Every decision-maker is present
  • The prospect states ≥4 funnel numbers unprompted
  • The prospect asks for price or a start date
  • You have a 10-minute snapshot with two specific holes named
  • Production has confirmed a kickoff window

Even on a compressed call, don't hand over the positioning answer, the brand story or a DIY manual.

The economics calculator

Open the calculator. Tag every input as theirs, estimated or benchmark.

Inputs

  • Monthly ad spend
  • CPC or cost per lead (never invent both)
  • Junk rate: spam, job seekers, wrong area
  • Booking rate = appointments set ÷ real leads
  • Close rate = jobs sold ÷ appointments run
  • Service ticket, install ticket, service/install mix
  • Gross margin (if known)
  • Optional lifetime: replacement rate, replacement ticket, membership rate, membership value

Outputs

  • Cost per real lead
  • Cost per booked job
  • Cost per acquired customer
  • First-job contribution, labeled on screen: "First job only. Doesn't include replacement, membership or referral revenue. This doesn't mean the channel loses money."
  • Lifetime view (only when the lifetime fields are filled): expected gross profit per customer, contribution after acquisition, profit per ad dollar
  • Scenario block, stamped "Assumption, not a forecast", plus a 3×3 booking × close sensitivity grid

Locking rule. If more than 2 core inputs are benchmarks, the dollar-outcome cells lock. Untagged inputs count as benchmarks. You can show the structure but not quote a monthly figure. Call review fails any recording where locked cells were talked around.

CPC rule. The post-click rates are the expensive leak. Never promise "we'll save you $50 a click." If month-three CPC is $48 and the booking rate moved, the engagement succeeded.

Customer value framing

Don't default to "you're losing money on every job." Experienced owners run thin first-job contribution on purpose. The service call is the door. The replacement is the business.

When first-job contribution is negative and the lifetime fields are empty: don't moralize. Ask:

That's if a customer is only ever worth one ticket. You know that's not true. What's a customer worth to you over three years?

Let the owner supply the number. A number they give you is a number they believe.

Persona routing: delegator vs. controller

Delegator signals
  • "We hired an agency, they couldn't run it"
  • Has a bookkeeper, thinks in hours
  • Says "we" about the company
Here's everything that has to go right. We own almost all of it. Your part is a kickoff, two review rounds, and approvals within five business days, starting [production-confirmed date].
Controller signals
  • "I did it myself but got busy"
  • Still quotes their own jobs
  • Says "I" about the company
You built this company. You could absolutely build this pipeline. The piece owners in your position usually skip is the competitive and housing-stock research. That's what decides whether the mark is ownable or just another blue wrench. Here's what getting that piece wrong costs per month on the numbers we just ran. You approve the positioning before a single sketch. Nothing goes on a truck you didn't sign.

If you can't identify the persona, don't use either close. Book the next meeting.

AI / Fiverr objection

Concede first.

AI's fine for a mood board. Keep what you like as a reference.

Then apply the commercial test.

What AI can't know is what your three competitors already own in [city], what a 30-year-old housing stock means for what homeowners fear, and which promise nobody's claiming. Design is the small part. Research and consistent rollout across every touchpoint is the expensive part. A generic mark doesn't fail because it's ugly. It fails because it's forgettable, and forgettable means you compete on price.

Never say:

  • "AI looks cheap"
  • "You won't own your logo"
  • "You can't trademark an AI logo"

Legal accuracy (keep it narrow):

  • Purely AI-generated images have weaker copyright protection
  • Lookalike risk is real, because models are trained on existing marks
  • Many AI files aren't true vectors and may not print on wraps
  • "Ask your attorney about copyright and lookalike risk before this goes on ten trucks."

Competitor positioning

KickCharge (premium tier)

Comparable full-scope brand engagements from national specialists commonly run $20K+. Here's our scope next to theirs.

Use this as a floor anchor only. Verify the pricing quarterly. Never claim "same exact work."

Why is Fortitude's price lower? The guide can't invent this. The founder supplies one true sentence. Options:

  • "We're pricing to earn our first ten case studies. This pricing ends after client #10." (Only if true, with a real clock.)
  • "Founder-led delivery, no national overhead." (Invites "what happens when you grow?")
  • "Our scope excludes [specific line items]." (Needs a scope table with the thinner rows marked.)

Until that sentence exists, reps show Fortitude's scope line by line and offer to set the competitor's proposal beside it.

Cheaper competitors

Don't name them. Don't mention lawsuits unless you can produce the filing.

Ask every agency to show you the market research they do before they design anything, show you how the strategy connects to your specific market, and guarantee originality and ownership in writing.

The contractual move (stronger)

Put originality, file transfer and ownership in Fortitude's contract. Cap indemnity at fees paid. Exclude client-supplied assets. Then:

We put original IP and full file ownership in the agreement. Ask everyone you're talking to for the same in writing.

Fix the MSA before reps promise this.

Objection bank (drill these)

Turn on drill mode at the top to hide the answers. Say yours out loud, then click to check.

"That's a lot of money."
It is. Compared to what: another quote, your budget, or doing nothing? Because doing nothing has a number too. It's the one on the screen. If your cost per closed job is $X and your average ticket is $Y, you're paying $Z every time you win a job. The question isn't whether [price] is a lot. It's whether $Z per job is more or less.

Only use $ figures the calculator hasn't locked.

"I already have a logo."
Good. I'm not here to take it away. The question isn't whether you have a mark. It's whether that mark is doing any work. Most aren't, because there was no research behind them. If yours is strong and the strategy is sound, we'll tell you and sell you the rollout instead. That happens sometimes.
"AI is good enough now."
For a mood board, sure. Here's the test: ask it what a homeowner in [specific ZIP] with a 34-year-old house is most afraid of when their AC dies in July, and what your three closest competitors have already claimed. It'll give you something plausible and generic. Generic means invisible, and invisible means price competition.
"I get all my work from word of mouth."
Then you're leaving money on the table in a specific way. Word of mouth doesn't scale past your personal network, and it doesn't help you hire. When you're ready to add trucks, word of mouth won't fill them. Also, the person your customer refers is going to Google you before they call. What do they see?
"I tried an agency and it didn't work."
That's the most common thing I hear. Usually it's one of two reasons: nobody set up tracking, so nobody could prove anything, or they sold tactics without strategy. What happened?

Listen all the way through. Address the specific failure: tracking, ownership, communication or results.

"I need to think about it."
Fair. Usually when someone says that, there's one specific thing: the price, the timing, whether it'll work, or something else. Which one is it for you?
"I need to talk to my partner / spouse."
Of course. What do you think their biggest question will be?

Address it now. Then:

Would it help if I jumped on a 15-minute call with both of you to answer anything directly?
"Can we do it cheaper / smaller?"
Yes: the audit or the capture fix. It won't build the brand, but it'll show you how we work. Then we do the brand in the shoulder season, when you have the bandwidth.
"Why now? Let's revisit next year."
Two reasons. First, your competitor doesn't stop. Second, your seasonality: the next natural window is [month]. If we start in [month − 2], you're live by the season. Starting in the season means we're building while you're drowning.
"Why not just go with KickCharge?"
They do strong work. Comparable full-scope engagements from national specialists commonly run $20K+. Here's our scope line by line. Bring their proposal and we'll put it right next to ours, so you can see exactly what's in each.

The directive's version said "comparable strategic depth at roughly half" and attributed the gap to "overhead." That's a parity claim the Claims Register prohibits, plus a price explanation the founder hasn't supplied yet. Restore the overhead line only if it becomes the founder's approved sentence.

"Why not someone cheaper?"
Ask them three questions: show me the research you do before design, show me how the strategy connects to my market, and put originality and ownership in writing. If they can't answer, you're not saving money.

Do-not-promise list

  • No booking-rate, revenue, lead-count or ranking guarantees
  • No "we'll get you to #1 on Google"
  • No implying that a brand alone generates calls
  • No CPC miracles ("we'll save you $50 a click")
  • No competitor legal claims without verified filings
  • No unverified competitor pricing
  • No trademark-law opinions beyond "ask your attorney"
  • No indemnity language counsel hasn't written yet
  • No delivery dates production hasn't confirmed
  • No "millions" or "$100K/month" unless derived from their numbers

Training and certification

Measured on call review

  • Discovery talk ratio: rep <40%
  • ≥4 of the 6 funnel numbers captured
  • Prospect's own words read back
  • Partner invited to Call 2 (full engagements)
  • Dated next step on every call: 100%, no exceptions
  • No locked calculator cells talked around

Certified to run diagnosis solo when they can

  • Build the economics worksheet live on prospect numbers without error
  • Defend every assumption
  • Stay under 40% talk ratio in discovery
  • Correctly identify delegator vs. controller
  • Leave with a dated next step

The 6 funnel numbers (the directive cited "6" without listing them): ① monthly spend or cost per lead, ② booking rate, ③ close rate, ④ service ticket, ⑤ install ticket and mix, ⑥ gross margin.

Ramp: how long depends on who Taylor and Layla are. A rep closing 8% needs scripts and gates. A rep closing 25% needs the diagnostic framework and permission to deviate.
